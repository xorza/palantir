# Showcase reel and theme transition

Facts for the reel example and the theme transition, 2026-11-01.

## What palantir does today

- `Ui::set_theme_for` replaces the `Rc<Theme>` and bumps `theme_epoch`; the new theme "applies at
  once" (its doc). A scheme change does the same.
- A look animation (`StatefulLook` through `Ui::animate`, spec `Metrics::transition`, `FAST` =
  120 ms `OutCubic`) retargets when its target moves: a duration row restarts from `cur`
  (`MotionRow::retarget`), then advances one `dt` in the same tick (`MotionRow::advance`). So on
  a scheme change, chromes that animate their look fade, while text, panels, the window clear and
  colors an app reads from `Palette` snap.
- `RgbaF32`'s motion space is premultiplied linear (`to_motion` = `premultiplied`); its settle
  tolerance is 1/4096 linear.
- OKLab exists: `Interpolation::Oklab` for gradients, Okhsv for the color picker.
- A retarget follows CSS Transitions' reversing-adjusted start for a duration and keeps the
  velocity for a spring (`animation_row.rs` module doc).
- `#[animate(snap)]` takes the target's value for any `t` in `lerp` (`palantir-anim-derive`).
- No subtree theme: `Ui::theme` is the window's one theme.
- `Scroll::set_offset` and `Scroll::set_zoom` place a pan-zoom view from outside.
- `Layer` paints `Main`, `Popup`, `Modal`, `Menu`, `Tooltip`, `Debug`; `Debug` paints last and is
  hit-tested first.
- `Harness::shot` runs frames until the picture lands, each moving the clock one `frame_dt`;
  `Harness::play` and `settle` run frames after each action. `read_back` is under `internals`.

## Measured: a look behind a moving target

A duration row retargeted every frame is exponential smoothing with `α = ease(dt / secs)`. For a
target at constant speed, the steady delay is `(1 − α) / α · dt`.

| Spec | Rate | α | Delay |
|---|---|---|---|
| 120 ms `OutCubic` (a look) | 60 Hz | 0.3615 | 29.4 ms |
| 120 ms `OutCubic` (a look) | 120 Hz | 0.1942 | 34.6 ms |
| 300 ms `InOutCubic` (a theme, one set a frame) | 60 Hz | 0.000686 | 24.3 s |
| 300 ms `OutCubic` (a theme, one set a frame) | 60 Hz | 0.158 | 89 ms |

`InOutCubic` starts with `4t³`, so a restart every frame nearly stops the value.

## Theme transitions elsewhere

- Flutter: `MaterialApp` animates every change of `theme`, `darkTheme` or `themeMode` through
  `AnimatedTheme`; `themeAnimationDuration` defaults to `kThemeAnimationDuration` (200 ms),
  curve linear; `themeAnimationStyle` overrides both
  (<https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/material/MaterialApp/themeAnimationDuration.html>,
  <https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/material/AnimatedTheme-class.html>). `Theme` snaps,
  `AnimatedTheme` animates.
- Flutter `ThemeData.lerp`: blended fields use their own `lerp`; booleans, enums and other
  fields that cannot blend take `t < 0.5 ? a : b`
  (<https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/material/ThemeData/lerp.html>).
- Flutter `Color.lerp`: a `null` end interpolates from a transparent instance of the other color
  (<https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/dart-ui/Color/lerp.html>). `BoxShadow.lerp`: a `null` end
  interpolates from a shadow of the other's color with zero offset and blur
  (<https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/painting/BoxShadow/lerp.html>).
- Flutter implicit animations start each new tween from the current value
  (<https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/widgets/ImplicitlyAnimatedWidgetState/didUpdateTweens.html>);
  the first build sets the tween's begin to the target and starts no animation
  (<https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/widgets/ImplicitlyAnimatedWidgetState-class.html>).
- Telegram: screenshot the window, switch the theme under it, shrink the screenshot with a
  circular reveal
  (<https://nutrient.io/blog/change-android-themes-with-circular-reveal-animation>).
- UIKit made animations additive in iOS 8: a new animation adds its delta to the model value and
  the running one continues (WWDC 2014 session 236, <https://wwdcnotes.com/notes/wwdc14/236>).

## Color interpolation

- CSS Color 4 interpolates in Oklab by default, with premultiplied alpha; legacy sRGB syntaxes may
  interpolate in gamma-encoded sRGB for compatibility
  (<https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/CSS/color-interpolation-method>). The CSSWG reverted
  making legacy-color gradients Oklab by default (issue #7948, June 2025,
  <https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-css-archive/2025Jun/0142.html>).
- Flutter's `Color.lerp` interpolates the encoded sRGB values.
- Measured: a straight line in OKLab between two in-gamut sRGB colors can leave the gamut. Over
  every pair of the 27 colors with linear channels in {0, 0.5, 1}, sampled at t = k/20, the
  largest excursion is red (1, 0, 0) to white at t = 0.55: linear (1.110, 0.407, 0.333), 11 %
  over in red. Gamut mapping in CSS Color 4 is a separate step, in OKLCH.
- Measured: over all 50,135,040 one-code steps of one channel of 8-bit sRGB, the shortest in
  Oklab is 7.011e-5, red 9 → 10 on full green and blue (log2 −13.80); near black a step is up to
  0.067 (grey 0 → 1). Linear light is the opposite: its narrowest step is near black, 3.0e-4.
- Measured: a premultiplied Oklab tween whose alpha overshoots toward 0 paints far past white.
  Opaque red to blue at alpha 0.05 under an overshooting ease: at t = 1.05 (alpha 0.0025) the
  painted blue is 3.23, at t = 1.052 (alpha 0.0006) it is 50.6; premultiplied linear paints 0.05
  there. The cube in Oklab's inverse turns the division by alpha into `1/α²`.
- `RgbaF16::new` is the one clamp: alpha into 0..=1 and channels to at least 0; channels above 1
  stay, as HDR.

## Motion

- Minimum jerk (Flash and Hogan 1985): a point-to-point reach minimizes the integrated squared
  jerk; each axis follows `x0 + (x1 − x0)(10τ³ − 15τ⁴ + 6τ⁵)`. Human reaches have a peak to mean
  speed ratio near 1.75, as the model predicts
  (<https://nbviewer.jupyter.org/github/demotu/BMC/blob/master/notebooks/MinimumJerkHypothesis.ipynb>).
- Smooth pan and zoom: van Wijk and Nuij, "Smooth and efficient zooming and panning", InfoVis
  2003, an optimal camera path under perceived velocity; Reach and North fix its unsmooth start
  and end and make it interruptible (<https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.09358>).
- Zoom canvases cause motion sickness when overused; practitioners advise slow transitions and
  few zoom levels
  (<https://www.tutkit.com/en/text-tutorials/13566-using-prezi-effectively-tips-to-avoid-motion-sickness>).
- Keynote's Magic Move works best when two slides share an object
  (<https://support.apple.com/guide/keynote-iphone/tanff5ae749e/ios>).
- Screen Studio records the cursor track apart from the screen and draws the cursor again,
  smoothed, with motion blur on cursor, zoom and pan
  (<https://www.screen.studio/guide/animations-motion>).

## Encoding and delivery

- ffmpeg converts RGB to YUV with BT.601 matrices unless told otherwise, so a file tagged BT.709
  shows shifted colors; convert with `scale=out_color_matrix=bt709` and tag `-colorspace bt709
  -color_primaries bt709 -color_trc bt709`
  (<https://academysoftwarefoundation.github.io/EncodingGuidelines/Quickstart.html>).
- H.264 needs even width and height; `-pix_fmt yuv420p` for playback; `-movflags +faststart`
  puts the index first (<https://avpres.net/FFmpeg/sq_H264.html>).
- YouTube's recommended settings list frame rates up to 60 (48/50/60 as high frame rate), H.264
  High, 4:2:0; 60 fps SDR bitrates: 1080p 12 Mbps, 1440p 24 Mbps, 2160p 53–68 Mbps
  (<https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171>).
- Scaling encoded sRGB values as if they were linear darkens fine bright detail on a dark ground;
  the fix is to linearize, scale and encode again (Eric Brasseur, "Gamma error in picture
  scaling", <http://www.ericbrasseur.org/gamma.html>; KDE bug 228934,
  <https://bugs.kde.org/show_bug.cgi?id=228934>).
- H.264 levels by macroblocks a second: 5.1 983,040 (1920×1080 to 120.5 fps), 5.2 2,073,600,
  6 4,177,920 (3840×2160 to 128.9 fps)
  (<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Video_Coding#Levels>). So 1080p120 (979,200) fits
  5.1, 1440p120 (1,728,000) needs 5.2, and 2160p120 (3,888,000) needs 6.
- A recording pipeline hides readback latency with a ring of staging buffers: frame N maps while
  N + 1 renders (`ustreamer-capture`,
  <https://docs.rs/ustreamer-capture/latest/src/ustreamer_capture/staging.rs.html>).
- Chrome always allows muted autoplay; with sound only after an interaction or a high media
  engagement score (<https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay>).
